Constraint Logic Programming (CLP) is a mixture of constraint solving and logic programming. 

# Syntax
- atom: expression p(t1, . . . ,tn), with predicate symbol p/n 
- atomic constraint: expression c(t1, . . . ,tn), with n-ary constraint symbol c/n
- constraint: 
	- atomic constraint, 
	- or conjunction of constraints 
- goal: $\top$ (top), or ⊥ (bottom) or
	- atom, or an atomic constraint, or 
	- conjunction of goals 

- (CL) clause: A ← G, with atom A and goal G 
- CL program: finite set of CL clauses

# CLP vs LP
- ___generate-and-test___ in LP: impractical, facts used in passive manner only 
- ___constrain-and-generate___ in CLP: use facts in active manner to reduce the search space (constraints) 
- combination of 
	- LP languages: declarative, for arbitrary predicates, non-deterministic 
	- constraint solvers: declarative, efficient for special predicates, deterministic 

- combination of search with constraints solving particularly useful
