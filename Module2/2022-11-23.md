Suppose we want to prove that B is a logical consequence of
F = {A -> B, A}, i.e. that F $\vDash$ B. 

So, I can have many possibility to prove that:
1. Truth tables
2. Natural deduction

#### Natural deduction
If we want to prove this, we must use the _Modus Ponens_ rule (that is, the _elimination of the implication_). 

We have also another way of proving this, through _Refutation_. 

#### Refutation 
A literal is made of an atom and an operation. (i.e. ¬A, A $\lor$ B). 

To prove the previous formula, we tranform the implication in __clausal form__ (so that A => B becomes ¬A $\lor$ B). We obtain F':
$$
F' = \{¬A \lor B, A\}
$$
We then add the negation of the conclusion that we want to prove:
$$
F'' = {¬A \lor B, A, ¬B}
$$
\[explaining inconsistent rule..\]
Essentially we want to prove that if when we have a, the formula can be true only because of the value of B. 
[..]

Refutation calculus consists of simply this rule, the __empty clause rule__. Essentially, we must only look for a rule that is shown both negated and not negated.
